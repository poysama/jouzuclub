# Addressing People

{: .fs-9 }

Japanese rarely uses pronouns like "You" or "He/She". Instead, it relies on names and titles.
{: .fs-6 .fw-300 }

## Referring to Yourself (I/Me)

- **Watashi (私)**: Standard, gender-neutral polite.\n- **Boku (僕)**: Casual, used by males.\n- **Ore (俺)**: Very casual/rough, used by males.\n- **Atashi (あたし)**: Causal, used by females.

## Referring to Others (You)

Avoiding "You" (Anata) is best. Use the person's name followed by a title.
_田中さんは来ますか？(Tanaka-san wa kimasu ka?)_ (Will you come, Tanaka-san?)\n\n## Titles (Honorifics)\n\n- **-San (さん)**: The default title. Used for equals or people you don't know well.\n- **-Kun (君)**: Used for boys or male subordinates.\n- **-Chan (ちゃん)**: Used for girls, children, or pets. Very cute/familiar.\n- **-Sama (様)**: Very respectful. Used for customers or deities.\n- **-Senpai (先輩)**: Used for senior colleagues or upperclassmen.\n- **-Sensei (先生)**: Used for teachers, doctors, and authors. Note: You call them just "Sensei" without the name, or "Name-sensei".
