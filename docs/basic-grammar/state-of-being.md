# Declaring the State of Being

{: .fs-9 }

In Japanese, the most basic sentence structure involves declaring what something _is_. This is often done using "da" (だ) or "desu" (です).
{: .fs-6 .fw-300 }

## The Copula "Da" (だ)

The word `da` acts like the English verb "to be" (is, am, are), but strictly speaking, it declares the state of existence.

### Example

```text
Person A: Are you a student?
Person B: 学生だ。 (Gakusei da.) - I am a student.
```

{: .note }

> Note that Japanese often omits the subject (like "I" or "You") when it is understood from context.

## The Polite Form "Desu" (です)

`Desu` is the polite version of `da`. It is used when speaking to strangers, superiors, or in formal situations.

### Example

```text
学生です。 (Gakusei desu.) - I am a student (polite).
```

## Comparisons

| Casual (Plain)      | Polite                  | Meaning           |
| :------------------ | :---------------------- | :---------------- | --- | ----------------- | --------------------- | -------------------- |
| 学生だ (Gakusei da) | 学生です (Gakusei desu) | (I) am a student. | \n  | 元気だ (Genki da) | 元気です (Genki desu) | (I) am well/healthy. |
